PCA, by hand

Principal component analysis finds the directions along which your data varies most, so you can describe it with fewer numbers. Play with the two demos below to see why that works.

Each dot is a sample with two features. Reshape the cloud, then rotate the purple axis yourself. Every dot is projected onto it (grey lines). A good axis keeps the projected points spread out and the grey lines short.

The recipe

  1. Standardize each feature (subtract the mean, usually divide by the standard deviation) so no feature dominates just because of its units.
  2. Compute the covariance (or correlation) matrix, which records how every pair of features moves together.
  3. Find eigenvectors and eigenvalues. Eigenvectors are the component directions, and eigenvalues are the variance along each.
  4. Sort by eigenvalue and keep the top k components.
  5. Project your data onto those k directions. You now have k features per sample instead of the original many.

Caveats: PCA only captures linear structure, it is sensitive to feature scaling, and high variance does not always mean useful for your prediction task.
