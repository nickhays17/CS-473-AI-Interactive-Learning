Kernel density estimation

A histogram asks which bin each point falls in. A kernel density estimate gives every point its own small bump and adds the bumps together, which produces a smooth curve of where the data is concentrated.

The recipe

For each point, draw a kernel (a small bump) centered on it. Scale each bump so its area is 1/n, then add them all up. The height of the total at any x is the estimated density there, and the total area stays exactly 1.

Bandwidth matters most

It sets how wide each bump is. Too small and the curve spikes at every point. Too large and real structure, like two separate clusters, melts into one hump. The kernel shape matters far less.

Things to try

Load “Two clusters” and slide h up until the two peaks merge. Then switch kernels at a fixed h. Finally, drag a single point far to the right and watch how only its own bump moves.
